A payment network doesn't swap tokens. It moves value between currencies and chains, at settlement size, under rules someone will ask you about later - and the awkward part is that the pricing, the counterparty, and the compliance evidence all have to hold at once.

Broad multi-chain aggregators deliver reach across the long tail; TetraFi is purpose-built for the narrower segment your flow actually lives in - [stablecoin corridors, institutional ticket sizes, identified counterparties](/why-tetrafi).

## What You Get

* **Corridors at size** - competing LP inventory outprices on-chain routing precisely where pools give out
* **Stablecoin-native coverage** - the USD, EUR, and regional-currency set, with issuer mint and burn rails composing as legs in the same route ([full list](/supported-chains))
* **Counterparties you can actually KYB** - jurisdiction filtering and approved LP rosters apply before a request ever reaches a counterparty, not as a rejection afterwards; a pool is not a legal entity, so every fill traces to a known, policy-enforced operator with a shared audit trail
* **One signature, any chain** - your user expresses an intent and signs once; routing, bridging, and settlement stop being your integration's problem
* **Firm before you commit** - each quote is signed and executable as-is, settled through escrow that delivers or refunds
* **Stop prefunding every venue** (coming soon) - [Venue Prefunding](/collateral/venue-prefunding) collapses one balance per CEX and OTC venue into a single credit layer: execute at flow size, fund at net size
* **Weekends and cutoffs stop being outages** (coming soon) - when an issuer's banking rails are shut, a [vault lane](/collateral/instant-redemption) keeps stablecoin redemption open
* **Corridors that net before they settle** (coming soon) - balanced north-south flow is exactly the shape that [nets well](/netting/where-it-applies): opposing flows offset each run, and only the residual pays a toll
