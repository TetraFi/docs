Applications reach the network from more than one direction. **Consumer apps and wallets** put firm professional liquidity behind a swap, so the shown price is the settled price. **Institutional apps** put a desk behind it - size, counterparty policy, portfolio moves that settle as one order. **Aggregators** pull that same institutional depth into a route set their pools cannot reach. One integration, three products - and the same rails, netting, and collateral network under all three.

## Consumer Apps & Wallets

For a consumer product, a swap has exactly one job: settle at the number on the screen. Every revert, every slipped price, every failed transaction chips away at trust that took months to earn. TetraFi routes your users onto firm professional liquidity, so the shown price is the settled price - reliably, invisibly.

### What You Get

* **Deepest where it counts** - stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets, priced by desks that actually hold them, not just majors
* **Zero-surprise settlement** - the price and the fill both hold, trade after trade
* **Consistent behavior everywhere** your users are, across all supported networks
* **Speed-or-price dial** - bias each quote toward whichever your UX values more
* **Monetization that rides along** - an optional fee inside each quote, nothing extra to build
* **Any chain in, one signature** - users move from whatever they hold into whatever you offer, with no bridge menus or multi-step flows in your UX
* **Yield-bearing balances that still feel instant, ahead:** [Instant Redemption](/collateral/instant-redemption) (coming soon), on the [Shared Collateral Network](/collateral), services exits from curated vault capital - a user holding a tokenized treasury or money-market position leaves at T+0, and your UX never has to explain a NAV window
* **A cheaper cost layer underneath, ahead:** [Multilateral Netting](/netting) (coming soon) offsets opposing flow each run so only the net residual pays a settlement toll - and retail-scale flows reach it through aggregation, without you integrating settlement infrastructure

## Institutional Apps

A retail swap widget stops working the moment a desk sits behind it: tickets grow, counterparty policies apply, and single-pair trades become portfolio operations. TetraFi is built for that reality - pricing that stays firm as size scales, quote flow you can restrict to approved counterparties, and multi-asset restructuring that settles atomically.

### What You Get

* **The assets your book actually holds** - regulated stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets, quoted at institutional size ([coverage](/supported-chains))
* **Firm at any size** - LP inventory is quoted, not modeled, so the number holds when the ticket grows
* **Your counterparty policy, enforced** - quote flow restricted to LPs you've approved
* **Portfolio moves as one atomic order** - baskets and rebalances across stablecoin and tokenized-asset positions, without transaction sprawl
* **Escrow-enforced outcomes** - delivery or refund, with stuck positions structurally impossible
* **Counterparties you can actually KYB** - a pool is not a legal entity; TetraFi routes to known, policy-enforced operators, filtered to the jurisdictions you allow, with a unified audit trail behind every fill
* **Rebalances that do not settle gross, ahead:** [Multilateral Netting](/netting) (coming soon) puts the network's obligations on one shared graph and clears them by **atomic multilateral set-off** - offsetting legs cancel and only your net residual moves, so a portfolio move pays the toll once rather than leg by leg
* **The wait stops costing you, ahead:** on the [Shared Collateral Network](/collateral) (coming soon), [Instant Settlement](/collateral/instant-settlement) fronts a T+N leg so your counterparty is paid today, and [Instant Redemption](/collateral/instant-redemption) gives T+0 exits on positions that settle on a NAV window

## Aggregators

Your router already knows every pool. What it's missing is the liquidity that never touches one: institutional inventory quoted firm. A single TetraFi integration drops that depth into your route set, and it shows up precisely where pools give out - on size, in [stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets](/supported-chains).

The same integration covers the harder case. The difficult part of a cross-chain route isn't the bridge - it's knowing what the far side will pay before you commit the near side. TetraFi locks both legs with firm commitments backed by real inventory, so the destination price is known at departure.

### What You Get

* **A roster, not a partnership grind:** one integration reaches every competing LP - no bilateral negotiations
* **Certainty you can route on:** firm quotes settle delivery-or-refund, so fills stick
* **Latency that keeps up** with a competitive routing loop
* **Depth where pools give out:** stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets quoted firm at size, on every supported network
* **Revenue without plumbing** - your fee is already inside the quote
* **Depth not capped by one desk's float, ahead:** [Solver Credit](/collateral/solver-credit) (coming soon) draws working capital from the [Shared Collateral Network](/collateral) into the fill itself - so the firm quote you route against is sized by the network's committed capital, not by what a desk happens to warehouse on that chain
* **Corridor cost compresses, ahead:** [Multilateral Netting](/netting) (coming soon) offsets opposing corridor flow each run so only the residual pays a settlement toll - the cost layer under the routes you already sell

### Across Corridors

Cross-chain routes get the same firm treatment, on both legs:

* **Escrow-backed depth on both sides** of the route, never best-effort
* **The far side is known at departure** - no post-bridge repricing risk
* **Corridor-agnostic integration** - one codepath, every supported network
