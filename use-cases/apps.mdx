**Consumer apps and wallets** put firm professional liquidity behind a swap, so the shown price is the settled price. **Institutional apps** put a desk behind it - size, counterparty policy, portfolio moves that settle as one order. **Aggregators** pull that same institutional depth into a route set their pools cannot reach. One integration, three products - all reaching the same [stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets](/supported-chains), priced by desks that actually hold them, on every supported network. **Agent flows** sign the same intent under a bounded mandate - and, ahead, many small agent payments [net down](/netting) to one residual (coming soon).

## Consumer Apps & Wallets

For a consumer product, a swap has exactly one job: settle at the number on the screen. Every revert, every slipped price, every failed transaction chips away at trust that took months to earn.

### What You Get

* **Zero-surprise settlement** - the price and the fill both hold, trade after trade
* **Speed-or-price dial** - bias each quote toward whichever your UX values more
* **Monetization that rides along** - an optional fee inside each quote, nothing extra to build
* **Any chain in, one signature** - users move from whatever they hold into whatever you offer, with no bridge menus or multi-step flows in your UX
* **Exits that don't wait for the issuer** (coming soon) - a holder leaves a tokenized treasury or money-market position T+0, and redeems a stablecoin when the issuer's banking rails are shut; [vault capital](/collateral/instant-redemption) funds the wait, not your treasury

## Institutional Apps

A retail swap widget stops working the moment a desk sits behind it: tickets grow, counterparty policies apply, and single-pair trades become portfolio operations.

### What You Get

* **Firm at any size** - LP inventory is quoted, not modeled, so the number holds when the ticket grows
* **Your counterparty policy, enforced** - quote flow restricted to LPs you've approved
* **Portfolio moves as one atomic order** - baskets and rebalances across stablecoin and tokenized-asset positions, without transaction sprawl
* **Escrow-enforced outcomes** - delivery or refund, with stuck positions structurally impossible
* **Counterparties you can actually KYB** - a pool is not a legal entity; TetraFi routes to known, policy-enforced operators, filtered to the jurisdictions you allow, with a unified audit trail behind every fill
* **Settle net, not gross** (coming soon) - a desk's opposing trades clear by [set-off](/netting) and only the residual moves, so inventory stays where it sits; a [T+N leg](/collateral/instant-settlement) is paid today

## Aggregators

Your router already knows every pool. What it's missing is the liquidity that never touches one: institutional inventory quoted firm. A single TetraFi integration drops that depth into your route set, and it shows up precisely where pools give out - on size, in stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets.

The difficult part of a cross-chain route isn't the bridge - it's knowing what the far side will pay before you commit the near side. TetraFi locks both legs with firm commitments backed by real inventory, so the destination price is known at departure - one codepath, every supported network.

### What You Get

* **A roster, not a partnership grind:** one integration reaches every competing LP - no bilateral negotiations
* **Depth that isn't one desk's float** (coming soon) - [Solver Credit](/collateral/solver-credit) puts network liquidity on the destination chain, so size at the far end isn't capped by any single LP's inventory
