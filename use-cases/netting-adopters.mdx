The clearing engine is **domain-blind by construction**: it clears who owes whom and by when, so a new kind of obligation enters through an intake lane and needs no engine changes at all.

Netting is not a venue you have to bring flow to, and it is not waiting on TetraFi to reach critical mass first - it is **adoptable by operators who run none of TetraFi's other rails**. Set-off is added on top of the rails you already have rather than replacing them, and TetraFi's own rails are simply the first book pointed at it, not the only one. It is privacy-preserving by construction: positions are readable only inside a [sealed engine](/netting/privacy-and-control) and you see only your own slice, so a book plugs in without leaking its flow, its counterparties, or its edge to anyone - TetraFi included.

## What Your Book Gets

Whatever the book - a loan book, a receivables ledger, a payment schedule - the outcome is the same three things:

* **Less to settle.** Obligations between the same participants collapse by **atomic multilateral set-off** to one net residual per party, so a day's gross flow stops paying gross tolls - and every other book that joins the graph compresses yours further
* **Capital efficiency: less capital clears more debt.** Only the net residual ever needs funding, so the same capital clears a far larger gross book - and the gap is widest in a high-volatility event, when gross settlement demands the most liquidity at the moment it is scarcest. In between, hold, don't move: no pre-funding, no margin, no inventory bridged to cover a debt
* **The residual, handled.** Whatever doesn't net takes a priced backstop from the [Shared Collateral Network](/collateral), with an early exit any time before the deadline - so the worst case is what you pay today, and the remainder stops being your problem

## Adopt It On What You Already Run

You configure your participation, not the engine. There is one clearing network - the way there is one Ethereum - and what you own is your slice of it:

* **Your own policy domain** - your counterparty sets, deadline-tier menu, backstop configuration, funding mode, perimeter policy, and visibility slice
* **Your own backend, or ours** - the clearing service is packaged to run on your institution's own infrastructure, or alongside a backend you already operate
* **Your existing settlement, untouched** - set-off sits on top of the rails you run today; what changes is how much has to move at the end of a run, not who moves it

It is the shape Morpho gave lending: nobody forks the protocol - you run your own market on one shared, immutable core, and that shared core is where the network effect lives.
