## One Pool, Every Settlement Gap

Institutional settlement runs on waiting: T+1 desk cycles, NAV windows, venue prefunding, cross-chain fill latency. Today each wait is financed privately - idle buffers, credit lines, solver balance sheets - capital parked once per product and dead the rest of the time.

The Shared Collateral Network finances all of them from one place. **Capital is committed once, into permissioned [programmable vaults](/collateral/vaults) with identifiable counterparties, and every use case reaches that capital through an adapter - never as a new pool to fund.** What replaces the parked buffers is **enforceable commitments**: capital moves only against a verified obligation, with the proof of how it comes back checked in the very transaction that draws it. The layer that builds this is programmable collateral; its founding product is programmable credit - financing the gap between when value is promised and when it settles.

## Why the Capital Stays

* **Stickiness is opportunity density, not lock-up.** Between draws the collateral stays productive; every product added to the network is another reason for the same capital to stay put.
* **Underwrite once, reuse everywhere.** A homemade pool has to be analysed from scratch every time. Here the vault is analysed once, every product added inherits that analysis, and deviation - not everything - is what stands out.
* **Compliance is native.** KYB'd counterparties, jurisdiction filters, and machine-checkable policy on every draw - the same enforcement that gates trading on TetraFi, not a bolt-on.
* **TetraFi is infrastructure.** Every vault names its own regulated operator facing depositors and drawers. TetraFi runs the rails, originates the first obligations, and is the network's first customer.
