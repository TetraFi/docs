TetraFi works with four kinds of account. They differ in one thing: **who can move the funds**. Whatever the kind, **TetraFi never moves funds without the owners' approval**, or a limit they approved.

| Category | What it means | Set it up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **External wallet** | You hold the key. It signs only when you connect it. | [External wallets](/wallets/external-wallets) |
| **Hosted wallet** | A provider runs the key. It can't move funds without your owners' approval or a limit they set. | [Turnkey](/wallets/hosted-turnkey) · [Privy](/wallets/hosted-privy) · [Fireblocks](/wallets/connect-fireblocks) |
| **Custody account** | Someone else holds the funds and moves them on your instruction: a qualified custodian, or your account at a venue. | [Custody accounts](/wallets/custody-accounts) |
| **Bank account** | Your company's own bank account. TetraFi shows no bank balance and moves no money from it. | **Treasury › Accounts** |

Treasury › Accounts shows one section per category.

<Accordion title="What TetraFi does with each">
| Category | Who can move the funds | What TetraFi does with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **External wallet** | You. The key is on your own device (a browser extension, mobile or hardware wallet, or an address you paste). Only you can move funds. | Sends only when you connect and sign. Everything else is monitored only. |
| **Hosted wallet** | Your owners. The key is secured by a provider, shown as "Runs on" its provider, such as "Runs on Privy". Funds move only with your owners' approval or a limit they set. One owner ("just you") is the simplest case. | Prepares requests; your owners approve them. With one owner, it acts in that person's own session, or through a limited grant they approve. |
| **Custody account** | A custodian or a venue, on your instruction, shown as "Held by" the custodian or "Held at" the venue, such as "Held by BitGo Trust" or "Held at Binance". | Sends instructions and reads balances. The custodian's or venue's own approvals still apply. |
| **Bank account** | Your company, at its bank. | Records it as a payout destination. A bank account never sends without a person present. |

Exchange and OTC venue accounts are custody accounts held at the venue, and bank accounts are their own kind. Neither is a wallet.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Every field and value">
| Field | Answers | Values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scope** | Who owns it | **Organisation**: the company's wallet, the one that faces LPs and brokers. **Personal**: one member's wallet. |
| **Shared** | Who else may use it | Organisation wallets are always workspace-wide. Sharing a personal wallet with chosen members (view and request, nothing more) is rolling out. |
| **Category** | Who can move the funds | **External wallet**, **Hosted wallet**, **Custody account** or **Bank account**. |
| **Key technology** | How the key signs | MPC, secure enclave, HSM, threshold network, a key the provider manages, or an external key TetraFi knows nothing about. A contract account has no key of its own; each of its owners has one. |
| **Account type** | What the chain sees | An ordinary address, an address with EIP-7702 smart features (shown as **Smart (7702)**), a multisig contract, a modular smart account, or a ledger account at a custodian. |
| **Wallet type** | The label you see | The smart-account kind for a contract account, otherwise the key technology and provider, such as "MPC · Fireblocks". |
| **TetraFi role** | What TetraFi can do | **None**, or **bounded delegate** ([below](#who-controls-the-funds)). |
| **Link** | Is ownership proven | **Linked** or **Not linked** ([below](#linked-or-not-linked)). |

Scope and category are independent: every category exists in both scopes.
</Accordion>

## Whose provider account

A hosted wallet lives in an account at its provider. Each wallet shows whose, for example "Runs on Privy · Provided by TetraFi" or "Runs on Turnkey · Your account *Treasury ops*".

| Connection | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Provided by TetraFi** | TetraFi runs the account at the provider, so you need no provider contract. The wallet's owners are still your people. TetraFi's account holds no owner role. |
| **Bring your own account** | You connect your own provider account in **Treasury › Providers**, and wallets are created in it. |

Privy is available both ways. Turnkey is available with your own account; Turnkey provided by TetraFi is rolling out. Where an option is not set up on an environment, the create form shows it as not offered.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Switching where new wallets go">
    Every wallet is pinned to the provider and connection it was created with. The workspace's choice decides only where **new** wallets go. Changing it never moves an existing wallet. Every earlier wallet keeps reading balances and sending on its own provider.

    When existing wallets would stay behind, a switch needs your acknowledgement. Without it, the switch is refused and nothing changes. The refusal lists, for each provider and connection, how many organisation wallets and members' personal wallets stay there. Switching between two connections of the same provider counts as a switch too. Members' personal wallets are counted, never named.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Moving and retiring a wallet">
    <span id="moving-a-wallet-to-another-provider"></span>
    To move a wallet's funds to another provider:

    1. Create a new organisation wallet on the provider you want.
    2. Use **Move funds** from the old wallet to the new one. This is one Run with a step per asset, and the network coin goes last. The wallet's owners approve it like any other send.
    3. Retire the old wallet once it is empty.

    A wallet can't be retired while it is holding a balance, part of an open Run, holding funds reserved for a payment, the source of an active grant, or the workspace's saved default. Each refusal names its cause. Network-coin dust smaller than one transfer's fee can't be sent, so it stays in the wallet and remains under its owners' control. A retired wallet keeps its history, but it can't send or receive again.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Removing a provider connection">
    A connection that any wallet or pending setup still uses can't be removed. The refusal counts what still uses it. To free the connection, move and retire the organisation wallets that use it, and each member removes their own empty personal wallets on it (only the member can do this). Once nothing uses the connection, it can be removed.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Who controls the funds

A hosted wallet's **owners** approve its payments. Together they form the **owner group**, and its **threshold** is how many owners must approve, such as 2 of 3. Each owner approves with their own sign-in methods, such as a passkey or an email code, or with one of their own wallets. The provider checks each approval, not TetraFi.

| Who | Can do alone |
| --- | --- |
| **The owners**, at the threshold | Approve a payment, change the owners, set limits |
| **The provider** | Run the key and enforce the owners' rules. It never moves funds on its own. |
| **TetraFi** | Read and prepare requests (role **None**), or sign within limits the owners approved (role **bounded delegate**) |

A wallet with one owner ("just you") is allowed. For company money, Treasury warns against it: if that person loses their sign-in methods, no payment can be approved.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Bounded delegates">
    The owners approve a limited signer, for example for an automation or an agent. The provider enforces its limits; a limit the provider can't enforce is refused, with the reason. Revoking it stops new sends.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Control changed">
    TetraFi checks who holds a wallet's native owner roles. A personal wallet's owners count as yours only when a complete read shows every factor as permanent and none of them as TetraFi's; a failed read, or a factor TetraFi can't attribute, reads as unread, never as yours. If any owner role is held by a TetraFi key, the wallet reads **Control changed** and every send from it is refused. The fix is in the provider: remove TetraFi's signing rights there.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Recovery share">
    A recovery share is the one other role TetraFi will ever accept, and it is rolling out. With it, TetraFi would hold one share of a wallet key for recovery only: it never signs alone, never counts toward a quorum, and the wallet stays under its owners' control. You would opt in per wallet.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Linked or Not linked

**Linked** means ownership is proven: by a signature from the wallet, or by the provider that runs it. Hosted wallets are linked when they are created. An external wallet is linked once you sign one message with it.

**Not linked** is an address you pasted without signing. It can receive and be watched, but it never sends, approves or counts as the holder. Link it later from its wallet page. See [External wallets](/wallets/external-wallets).

## Networks

A wallet has **one type** (EVM, Solana or Tron) and **one address** for that type. You switch networks of that type on and off; you never create a new wallet for a new network. See [Networks](/wallets/networks).

## What each provider supports

Turnkey and Privy wallets can be created and approved inside TetraFi; Fireblocks and BitGo send and sign by API.

<Accordion title="Capabilities by provider">
**Yes**: the provider enforces it. **Partial**: part of it works. **Monitored**: TetraFi checks it and refuses what breaks it, but the provider doesn't enforce it. **No**: not available today.

| Capability | Turnkey | Privy | Fireblocks | BitGo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Create the wallet from TetraFi | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Send and sign by API | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Sign typed data | Yes | Yes | Yes | Partial |
| Owner quorum | Yes | Yes | Partial | Partial |
| Approve inside TetraFi | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Per-transaction caps | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Rolling spend limits | Monitored | Monitored | Yes | Yes |
| Allow-list of destinations | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Bounded delegate (automations, agents) | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Expiry on a delegation | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Install a policy from TetraFi | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Read back the enforced policy | Yes | Yes | Yes | Partial |
| Freeze | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Outcome updates | Partial | Partial | Yes | Yes |
| Recovery | Provider | Provider | Provider recovery kit | Backup key |

Wallet gas sponsorship and transaction batching are not yet available for any provider. Safe multisigs and modular smart accounts are not yet available. Providers that are not yet available appear greyed out when you add a wallet.
</Accordion>

## The rule that holds everywhere

An API key, an admin session, a provider connection or a gas sponsor never creates wallet authority. Ownership is checked again before every send.

<Accordion title="Defaults and request IDs">
  Per-chain defaults are preferences, never authority: ownership is checked again before every send. Every wallet or grant request carries a request ID you generate, so a lost response reconciles under it and nothing is created twice.

  Where delegated signing enters the loop is on [Orders](/trade/orders); the same wallets move funds under policy on [Treasury & Custody](/core-concepts/treasury).
</Accordion>
