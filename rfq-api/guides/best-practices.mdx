Treat this page as the operational manual for the RFQ API. Each rule earned its place: real LPs have widened, de-prioritised, or denylisted integrators who broke it.

The desks quoting on TetraFi run their own risk systems. They watch flow per source and push back on patterns that look adversarial. Follow these six rules and your access to the liquidity surface stays stable; ignore them and you'll see fill rates sag, sporadic no-liquidity answers, and eventually LP-level bans.

## 1. Filter before you quote

Use the [price feed](/price-api/quickstart) for sizing and route estimation, and only call `/rfq/quotes` when the stream says the trade is worth pursuing.

Streamed prices give you continuous depth at no cost to LPs, while firm quotes are rate-limited per key precisely *because* each one is a priced commitment someone has to stand behind.

```python theme={null}
# Anti-pattern: firm-quote every candidate intent
quotes = httpx.post(QUOTES_URL, json=...)  # burns rate limit even when there's no liquidity

# Recommended: pre-filter on the stream
if stream_depth_for(corridor) >= intent_size:
    quotes = httpx.post(QUOTES_URL, json=...)
```

See [Estimating VWAP](/price-api/guides/vwap-estimation) for the canonical pattern.

## 2. Execute what you quote

A firm quote prices one moment in time. Holding a quote, watching the market, and submitting only when the stale price beats the live one is **toxic flow** - and it's visible.

**The clean pattern:** request quotes at the moment you intend to execute, and drive everything earlier in your pipeline off the streamed prices.

## 3. One intent, one quote

Slicing - splitting a single fill intent into several smaller quote requests - comes in two flavours, and LPs detect both:

* **Several quotes for one fill:** five `1M USDC → WETH` requests when you mean to fill `5M` once.
* **Several quotes for one execution:** two simultaneous `10M USDC → WETH` quotes, both executed - bundled or across consecutive blocks. Each was priced against the LP's full inventory; the first draws it down and the second fails. Blocks arrive seconds apart, inventory doesn't refill in that window - simultaneous requests are slicing however you submit them.

Need size flexibility? That's what [partial fills](/rfq-api/guides/partial-fills) are for: a 5M quote with `partialFill: true` can settle at 4.5M on the same firm terms - supported, not adversarial.

Partial fills also solve the AMM top-up case: when part of a trade routes through an AMM, its true output isn't known until execution. Quote the maximum you might need, let the order partially fill down to what the AMM leg actually produced, and skip a second round trip on the critical path.

```python theme={null}
# Anti-pattern: five small quotes for one intent
for _ in range(5):
    httpx.post(QUOTES_URL, json=intent_for("1000000"))

# Recommended: one quote, partial fill enabled
httpx.post(QUOTES_URL, json=intent_for("5000000", partial_fill=True))
# Settles at any size up to the quote, on the firm terms.
```

## 4. Quote only what's tradable

Probing corridors that aren't supported costs LP compute and marks you as an unfiltered taker. Make the pair list your gate:

```python theme={null}
import httpx

pairs = httpx.get(
    "https://api.tetrafi.io/api/v1/pairs",
    headers={"X-API-Key": KEY},
).json()["pairs"]

tradable = {(p["input"]["chainId"], p["input"]["address"].lower(),
             p["output"]["chainId"], p["output"]["address"].lower()) for p in pairs}

if (10, usdc_op.lower(), 8453, usdc_base.lower()) in tradable:
    quotes = httpx.post(QUOTES_URL, json=..., headers=...)
```

Any listed pair can be quoted against any other supported leg - the list is the canonical availability check.

## 5. Firm means firm

An RFQ quote is **executable at its stated terms until `validUntil`**, enforced by the escrow. When your router compares a TetraFi quote against AMM routes, don't haircut it with the "expected slippage" you'd apply to a pool price:

| Source              | Returned price     | What you actually receive               |
| ------------------- | ------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| TetraFi RFQ           | Firm, escrow-enforced | The quoted terms - or a refund       |
| AMM (Uniswap, etc.) | Mid-price estimate | Estimate minus slippage and MEV cost    |

Comparing a firm quote and a pool estimate as if they were the same kind of number systematically misroutes flow away from your best price.

## 6. Show us whose flow it is

Attribution fields tell TetraFi and the LPs who is really behind a request. They feed abuse prevention and per-source reputation, so accuracy directly shapes the pricing you receive:

| Field           | Send when                                                | Value                                                        |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `user`          | Always (required)                                        | The wallet that signs and funds the order                    |
| `directUserId`  | You're a broker acting for onboarded end-users           | The end-user's ID from your workspace's direct-user registry |
| `signerWallet`  | The signing wallet differs from the funding `user`       | The wallet that will actually sign                           |
| `metadata` source tag | You aggregate flow from multiple upstream sources  | A stable identifier per sub-source                           |

**Direct integrator** - `user` is the end-user's own wallet, nothing in between: the required field is all you need.

**Broker flows** - your users onboard through your workspace; send `directUserId` so screening and reputation attach to the actual person, not your whole integration.

**Aggregated flows** - tag each upstream source consistently in `metadata`. Reputation then accrues per sub-source, so one bad downstream partner can't taint the rest of your flow.

<Warning>
  **Attribute honestly.** Wrong or missing attribution blinds screening and reputation at the same time - the result is worse pricing for all of your flow and, in persistent cases, de-prioritisation.
</Warning>

## Pre-launch checklist

Before going live, confirm:

* The price stream - not firm quotes - drives your pre-trade filtering
* No quote is ever cached between issuance and execution
* One quote per intended fill; `partialFill` covers flexible sizing
* Only one quote per fill is ever executed
* Every quoted pair appears in `/api/v1/pairs`
* AMM comparisons are net of slippage; TetraFi quotes are not haircut
* Attribution fields reflect the true origin of the flow

An integration that holds all seven lines is the difference between stable access and one that quietly degrades over weeks.
