Every product in the [Shared Collateral Network](/collateral) is designed to run on the same kind of vault: a permissioned pool of committed capital with identifiable counterparties. Each use case will reach the capital through an **adapter**, code that encodes what the money may do and the proof of how it comes back, verified when the draw happens.

| Role | What it will do |
| --- | --- |
| Curator | Deploys a vault and owns its risk envelope. |
| Depositor | Supplies the vault's asset and holds vault shares. |
| Drawer | Instructs draws; the vault moves the money itself. |
| Another framework's vault | Joins with its own account, under its curator's mandate. |

At launch, vaults are planned to be TetraFi-curated with a whitelisted participant set; independent curation opens after.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The primitive">
    Add a product, and it arrives as a new adapter over the standing pool, never as a new pool to fund - which is the whole capital-efficiency case: one committed base earning across every use case, instead of a buffer per product earning nothing. Capital will be encumbered only while a draw is live, and only to that one obligation - the rest of the pool keeps working for every other product at the same time.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Curators">
    Curators will deploy vaults and own their risk envelope: which products and adapters are enabled, which assets qualify, the minimum discount or yield every draw must clear, per-drawer and per-asset caps, the withdrawal delay, and the compliance policy that gates everyone else. They earn a share of the realised spread.

    Curation is where vaults compete. Two curators can serve the same flow with different envelopes - one strict, single-jurisdiction, and short-duration; the other broader and higher-yielding - and depositors pick the envelope, not just the asset.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Depositors">
    Depositors will supply the vault's asset and hold vault shares against it. Yield comes from real settlement demand across every product the curator enables, not emissions - what depositors earn is what idle buffers used to cost the other side:

    - **The discount spread** - obligations are bought below oracle value and mature at face
    - **Intrinsic obligation yield** - where the obligation itself accrues, priced with duration
    - **Idle-capital base yield** - potentially, as a curator-optional strategy; an open design decision, since it adds external protocol risk

    Exits go through a withdrawal delay - the same window that caps how long any obligation may live, so a requested exit can always be honoured from maturing obligations. If an obligation ever impairs, the loss marks all shares down pro-rata immediately: nobody wins by racing the exit.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The demand side">
    Each product brings its own drawers to the same capital: market makers and solvers filling [redemptions](/collateral/instant-redemption), desks and issuers being fronted a [deferred leg](/collateral/instant-settlement), PSPs executing across [venues](/collateral/venue-prefunding), solvers funding [cross-chain fills](/collateral/solver-credit). Drawers instruct draws; the vault performs every movement itself, so a drawer never custodies vault capital.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Bring your own vault">
    Many allocators are mandated to deploy through a specific vault framework. They will still be able to join the network: a vault on another framework connects once and holds its own account inside the network, governed by its curator's mandate - which use cases it may fund, and how much of each.

    - **Your mandate, enforced** - capital reaches only the use cases the curator allowed, never above the caps it set
    - **Idle capital keeps working, at no extra fee** - the unused part earns in a short-term reserve and moves into a use case only at the moment a verified draw needs it
    - **Losses stay where you chose to go** - each use case tracks every source's share of it, so a loss in one never reaches a vault that did not allow it
    - **One network across frameworks** - several vaults, ours included, fund the same use cases side by side, so every committed dollar backs the whole network
    - **Money never leaves your vault, if your mandate says so** - a curator can instead commit a line: the capital stays put and is pulled only for a verified draw, then returned
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Who gates whom">
    Every role will pass compliance checks appropriate to it - KYB, sanctions, jurisdiction - against the vault's own policy, and draws re-check standing at execution time. Compliance is configuration, per vault: the same primitive can run a tightly-scoped regulated vault and a broader one side by side. Every vault names its own regulated operator facing depositors and drawers - TetraFi stays infrastructure.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
