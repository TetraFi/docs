**In one line:** one vault lends stablecoins to licensed payment companies through ring-fenced, secured facilities, against customer payments already in flight - so payouts go out now, and depositors earn from real cross-border payment flow.

## The Gap Behind Every Instant Payment

Every instant cross-border payment hides a funding gap. The customer is paid in seconds; the fiat that funds the payout arrives hours to days later over bank rails - on every corridor, every day. Payment companies close the gap by prefunding: their own capital parked in advance, sized for their worst day, in every corridor and with every payout partner. It earns nothing while it waits, and it caps how much flow they can serve.

## Credit Backed by the Payment Itself

PSP Prefunding replaces the parked capital with stablecoin credit drawn the moment a payout is needed, and repaid when the customer's fiat lands.

* **What backs each draw** - a customer payment that has already been sent, or approved prefunding with a named payout partner, repaid from the fiat the customer has already paid in, typically within T+0 to T+7.
* **Verified before capital moves** - the payment is evidenced and checked before the draw: it exists, it is eligible, it matches the draw, and it is not already financed. The draw carries that proof, so every dollar out is tied to a specific payment on its way in.
* **Secured where the money lands** - capital goes only to ring-fenced originator vehicles, never onto a balance sheet, with security over their receivables, proceeds and collection accounts.
* **First-party when it runs on TetraFi** - when the payout's fiat-to-stablecoin leg executes through TetraFi's RFQ, the payment is originated on the same settlement rail that funds it.

**Who it finances:** licensed payment companies and remittance firms settling their own customers' transfers. No consumer lending, no token incentives, no directional exposure to crypto prices.

## Why One Vault, Many Originators

A single originator has a strong pipeline but uneven demand month to month, so a vault dedicated to it sits idle half the time. One shared vault allocates across a curated network of originators - corridors, currencies and payout partners diversify each other - and whatever is not drawn waits in the liquidity reserve, earning.

* **Earned limits** - every originator starts small and grows only on clean, reconciled performance, up to a capped share of the vault.
* **Committed facilities** - originators draw within committed lines, against verified demand; when demand exceeds capital, ready-to-deploy, fairly priced originators come first, and repaid capital returns to the pool rather than rolling back to where it came from.
* **Priced for risk** - a draw is funded only when its yield, after expected loss and costs, clears the liquid alternative by a margin; otherwise capital stays in reserve.
* **Paused automatically** - a late repayment, stale data or a reconciliation break stops new draws for that originator.

## Built for Exits

* **Liquidity first** - near-term withdrawals plus a buffer are reserved before any capital reaches originators.
* **Cash from short, self-liquidating credit** - the reserve pays first; beyond it, financings settle in days, and every repayment goes to withdrawals before any new draw.
* **No one jumps the queue** - withdrawals are paid in order and in proportion, so no depositor, feeder or distributor exits ahead of another.

## What You Can Verify

Draws and repayments run through allowlisted contracts and are traceable on-chain, vault balances can be checked at any time, and NAV is published daily. Vault shares are composable receipt tokens that other protocols may accept as collateral at their own discretion.

## Not the Same as Venue Prefunding

[Venue Prefunding](/collateral/venue-prefunding) solves a different problem: capital sitting idle at every CEX and OTC venue a desk trades on. There is no customer payment behind those draws - they are secured by the venue balance they fund and by custodial enforcement. PSP Prefunding is narrower and tighter: every draw is backed by an identified payment already on its way.
