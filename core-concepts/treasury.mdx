Custody connections, vault accounts, wallets, transfers, and the written policy every transfer must clear. With an API key you read accounts, balances, Destinations and transfers, and you start, cancel, replace or retry a transfer; approvals stay with a person.

## What It Holds

Custody provider connections, registered in the dashboard only because they carry secret material. Under them: vault accounts with per-asset balances and history, your wallets, an address book.

## How wallets appear

**Accounts** has two tabs, **Company** and **Personal & shared**. Both show the same sections, one per [control](/core-concepts/wallets-and-signing#control-who-authorises-a-signature); the tab only filters by scope.

| Section | What it lists |
| --- | --- |
| **Governed wallets** | Wallets where a quorum of approvers authorises every signature. |
| **Embedded wallets** | Wallets one person's login controls. On **Company**, the section says to use a governed wallet for company money. |
| **Self-managed wallets** | Wallets you sign for by connecting them. |
| **Custodian accounts** | Accounts a regulated custodian holds. TetraFi instructs and reads. |
| **Not classified** | Wallets TetraFi can't place in a section, each with its reason and, when there is one, the fix. |

**Company** also lists your venue accounts and bank accounts, in their own sections. **Personal & shared** lists your own wallets. Sharing a personal wallet with other members is not yet available; when it is, its owner will decide who may view it and request from it, and nothing more.

Each row shows its wallet type and provider, such as "Secure enclave · Turnkey". A row also shows **Unverified** until its ownership proof, and **Control changed** when a TetraFi key could sign without your approvers. Neither can send.

**Add account**, then **Add a wallet**, asks how the wallet is controlled, then its type, then the provider. Providers that are not yet available are greyed out. A governed wallet is created with its owners: every proposed owner accepts before the provider sets it up.

## Moving Funds

One transfer resource. Source: a vault account or a registered wallet. Destination: a one-time address, one of your company's or linked wallets, a custodian-whitelisted wallet, or an OTC desk's deposit address. Send an idempotency key.

The policy is written, versioned, and can run in **shadow** before it enforces: a whitelist board, an approval quorum with an inbox, a velocity budget per principal, an approval window, every failure as data with retry or replace. The custodian's own approval is separate from the workspace's; both are shown. (Policy and statements rolling out.)

## Programmatic Access

Two scopes, `treasury_read` and `treasury_write`, behind two gates re-derived per request: the scope and the acting identity's live workspace permission. A refusal names which gate refused.

With a key you can list and read vault accounts and their history, refresh a balance, list and add Destinations (a person approves each one), and start, cancel, replace or retry a transfer. Every write takes an `Idempotency-Key`: send the same key again and you get the first answer back, never a second transfer. Approving a transfer or a Destination, changing policy and freezing need a person in the app. These Treasury operations join the public [API reference](/reference/introduction) with their error codes (rolling out).

## Desks and Corridors

Exchange and OTC desks are settlement lanes: deposit intents, T+1 balances, credit utilisation, an exception queue. Fiat corridors run through bank accounts per ramp provider (rolling out). A custodian ledger reconciles each statement window against your records within a fee tolerance you set.

Who may sign from which wallet is on [Wallets & Signing](/core-concepts/wallets-and-signing); delegating transfers inside a bounded envelope is on [Agents](/core-concepts/agents).
