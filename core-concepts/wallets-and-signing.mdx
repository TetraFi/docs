Every wallet stays under its owners' control. TetraFi never moves funds without the owners' approval, or a limited grant they approved. TetraFi describes each wallet with the same few fields, so you can see at a glance who controls it and what it can do.

## The fields on every wallet

| Field | Answers | Values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scope** | Who owns it | **Organisation**: the company's wallet, the one that faces LPs and brokers. **Personal**: one member's wallet. |
| **Shared** | Who else may use it | Organisation wallets are always workspace-wide. Sharing a personal wallet with chosen members (view and request, nothing more) is not yet available. |
| **Category** | Who can move the funds | **External wallet**, **Hosted wallet** or **Custody account** (below), plus **Bank account**. |
| **Key technology** | How the key signs | MPC, secure enclave, HSM, threshold network, a custodian-managed key, or an external key TetraFi knows nothing about. A contract account has no key of its own; each of its owners has one. |
| **Account type** | What the chain sees | An ordinary address, an address with EIP-7702 smart features (shown as **Smart (7702)**), a multisig contract, a modular smart account, or a custodian ledger. |
| **Wallet type** | The label you see | The smart-account kind for a contract account, otherwise the key technology and provider, such as "MPC · Fireblocks". |
| **TetraFi role** | What TetraFi can do | **None**, or **bounded delegate** (below). |
| **Link** | Is ownership proven | **Linked**: proven by a signature or by the provider. **Not linked**: a pasted or imported address before its proof. A wallet that is not linked can receive and be watched, but it never sends, approves or counts as the holder. |

Scope and category are independent: every category exists in both scopes. A personal hosted wallet can need several of its owner's factors, and a company can hold a hosted wallet with a single owner ("just you"), though Treasury warns against it: one person controls it, so give company money a hosted wallet with several owners.

## Category: who can move the funds

| Category | Who can move the funds | What TetraFi does with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **External wallet** | You. The key is on your own device (a browser extension, mobile or hardware wallet, or an address you paste). Only you can move funds. | Sends only when you connect and sign. Everything else is monitored only. |
| **Hosted wallet** | Your owners. The key is secured by a provider, shown as "Runs on" its provider, such as "Runs on Privy" (Turnkey, Privy, Fireblocks, BitGo, Safe). Funds move only with your owners' approval or a limit they set. One owner ("just you") is the simplest case. | Prepares requests; your owners approve them. With one owner, it acts in that person's own session, or through a limited grant they approve. |
| **Custody account** | A custodian or a venue, on your instruction, shown as "Held by" the custodian or "Held at" the venue, such as "Held by BitGo Trust" or "Held at Binance". | Sends instructions and reads balances. The custodian's or venue's own approvals still apply. |

A provider never moves funds on its own: it runs the key, and your owners decide.

Exchange and OTC venue accounts are custody accounts held at the venue, and bank accounts are their own kind. Neither is a wallet.

## TetraFi's role

TetraFi is infrastructure: it never signs, sends or acts for you on its own.

- **None.** TetraFi can read the wallet and prepare requests. Every signature needs the owners.
- **Bounded delegate.** The owners approve a limited signer, for example for an automation or an agent. The provider enforces its limits; a limit the provider can't enforce is refused, with the reason. Revoking it stops new sends.

TetraFi checks who holds a wallet's native owner roles. A personal wallet's owners count as yours only when a complete read shows every factor as permanent and none of them as TetraFi's; a failed read, or a factor TetraFi can't attribute, reads as unread, never as yours. If any owner role is held by a TetraFi key, the wallet reads **Control changed** and every send from it is refused. The fix is in the provider: remove TetraFi's signing rights there.

A recovery share is the one other role TetraFi will ever accept, and it is not yet available. With it, TetraFi would hold one share of a wallet key for recovery only: it never signs alone, never counts toward a quorum, and the wallet stays self-custodied. You would opt in per wallet.

## Where new wallets are created

A new organisation hosted wallet is created through one of two connections:

- **Provided by TetraFi.** TetraFi runs the account at the provider, so you need no provider contract of your own. The wallet's owners are still your members' factors. TetraFi's account holds no owner role and is at most a bounded delegate. Privy is available provided by TetraFi. Turnkey provided by TetraFi is coming and can't be chosen yet; until then, choosing it is refused and nothing changes.
- **Bring your own account.** You connect your own Turnkey or Privy account, and wallets are created in it.

Each wallet shows which one it uses, for example "Runs on Privy · Provided by TetraFi" or "Runs on Turnkey · your account *Treasury ops*".

### A wallet stays where it was created

Every wallet is pinned to the provider and connection it was created with. The workspace's choice decides only where **new** wallets go. Changing it never moves an existing wallet. Every earlier wallet keeps reading balances and sending on its own provider.

### Switching where new wallets go

When existing wallets would stay behind, a switch needs your acknowledgement. Without it, the switch is refused and nothing changes. The refusal lists, for each provider and connection, how many organisation wallets and members' personal wallets stay there. Switching between two connections of the same provider counts as a switch too. Members' personal wallets are counted, never named.

### Moving a wallet to another provider

Switching doesn't move money. To move a wallet's funds to another provider:

1. Create a new organisation wallet on the provider you want.
2. Use **Move funds** from the old wallet to the new one. This is one Run with a step per asset, and the network coin goes last. The wallet's owners approve it like any other send.
3. Retire the old wallet once it is empty.

A wallet can't be retired while it is:

- holding a balance;
- part of an open Run;
- holding funds reserved for a payment;
- the source of an active grant;
- the workspace's saved default.

Each refusal names its cause. Network-coin dust smaller than one transfer's fee can't be sent, so it stays in the wallet and remains under its owners' control. A retired wallet keeps its history, but it can't send or receive again.

### Removing a provider connection

A connection that any wallet or pending setup still uses can't be removed. The refusal counts what still uses it. To free the connection:

- move and retire the organisation wallets that use it;
- each member removes their own empty personal wallets on it (only the member can do this).

Once nothing uses the connection, it can be removed.

**Yes**: the provider enforces it. **Partial**: part of it works. **Monitored**: TetraFi checks it and refuses what breaks it, but the provider doesn't enforce it. **No**: not available today.

## The rule that holds everywhere

An API key, an admin session, a provider connection or a gas sponsor never creates wallet authority. Per-chain defaults are preferences, never authority: ownership is checked again before every send. Every wallet or grant request carries a request ID you generate, so a lost response reconciles under it and nothing is created twice.
