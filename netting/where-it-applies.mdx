The clearing engine is designed to be domain-blind: it nets who pays whom, how much, by when, whatever created the debt. So one clearing layer can serve very different books, and **every lane feeds the same graph, so each new kind of flow raises everyone's netting ratio.**

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What each lane nets">
    * **Trading & Payments** - cross-chain stablecoin obligations from trades on the network's rails, with compliance gates at every run entry.
    * **Agentic Netting** - agent spend across platforms nets to one residual per platform, paid out on the chain it chooses.
    * **Payment Networks** - retail-scale micro-flows aggregate into institutional obligations, clear against the network, then split back out per provider with full reconciliation.
    * **Tokenized Assets** - delivery-versus-payment pairs net with atomicity preserved: both legs settle in one unit or neither does.
    * **Risk Intelligence** - counterparty, concentration, and intraday liquidity views computed where the graph lives - each participant reads only its own.
    * **Borrowing & Lending** - repayments, interest, and margin calls between the same institutions settle net, freeing the most liquidity when markets turn.
    * **Receivables & Trade Finance** - dated invoice chains clear by settlement date, and an anchored invoice can't back two live obligations.
    * **Energy & Circular Debt** - a closed loop of debt - endemic in energy markets - is exactly what the engine extinguishes with zero money moved.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why the lanes compound">
    Netting finds value in opposing flow, and opposing flow lives across books, not inside them. A market maker's corridor rebalancing, an issuer's mints against redemptions, a payment network's north-south flows, a desk's T+1 obligations - each alone nets modestly; on one shared graph, each becomes the counterflow that clears the others. The ratio is a network property: more members, more lanes, more offsets, and the engine finds them all in one solve.

    That compounding is upside, not a precondition. A book nets against itself the day it joins, on the settlement rails it already runs - the shared graph only makes the residual smaller.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
