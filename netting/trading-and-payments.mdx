Every fill and every corridor payment pays the full toll - the bridge, the rebalance, the conversion - even when an equal and opposite flow crosses the network minutes later. Desks carry inventory on every chain to cover it, payment networks pre-fund every corridor to stay open, and the toll ends up as the floor under every spread.

## What Changes for You

* **A second firm quote, tighter.** Settle instantly at today's price, or [clear at a deadline you pick](/netting/how-it-works#two-ways-to-settle), at a tighter one. Same request, one more choice.
* **Hold, don't move.** Your obligations net against the network's opposing flow, so inventory stays where it already sits - no bridging a position across chains to cover a debt, no rebalancing round-trips.
* **One figure per asset and chain.** A day of fills or corridor payments settles as one net amount per asset and chain each run, on any chain you have configured - settlement decoupled from where the trade executed.
* **The first lane to ship.** Cross-chain stablecoin obligations are the first lane - the flows the network settles today, with compliance gates at every run entry.

## A Day of Flow

A desk, a payment network, and a market maker, one day, USDC:

* The Desk owes the payment network **500** - fills on its corridor
* The payment network owes the market maker **300** - corridor payouts
* The market maker owes the Desk **400** - fills on the other side

Gross, that is 1,200 USDC across three settlements. Netted, the loop of 300 cancels, and the rest settles along the original debts: the Desk pays the payment network 200, the market maker pays the Desk 100. **300 moves instead of 1,200.**

## How It Enters

Fills enter at quote time: request a **cleared** quote with a deadline tier, and the trade's settlement becomes an obligation. Corridor payments enter as **signed debts**. From there it is the standard [integration](/netting-api/integration#submit-obligations).
