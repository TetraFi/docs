Money is owed in circles, 30, 60, 90 days out: a supplier waits on a manufacturer, who waits on a distributor, who is owed by the supplier for logistics. Each invoice is paid in full on its own date, against invoices that would largely cancel. Netting is designed to clear the circle by settlement date. Lane: **Future**.

| You will get | How it is designed |
| --- | --- |
| Parked working capital back | Dated obligations clear together in the run before they fall due |
| Only debts you agreed to | A claim joins only once its debtor approves it |
| No double financing | One invoice backs one live obligation |

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What changes for you">
    * **The circle clears by settlement date.** Dated obligations clear together in the run before they fall due instead of wiring every leg, and the working capital parked against invoices that would have cancelled comes back.
    * **Nothing enters without the debtor.** A claim joins the graph only once its debtor approves it, line by line; an unapproved claim simply expires. No book is ever loaded with debts nobody agreed to.
    * **One invoice, one obligation.** Today the same invoice can be financed twice by lenders who never see each other. An obligation can anchor the hash of its originating document, so the same invoice can't back two live obligations in the same asset and chain.
    * **Your counterparties, unchanged.** Nothing is novated: each invoice stays between the two parties who signed it, on its own terms and its own date.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A day of flow">
    Three companies, invoices due the same week, USDC on Base:

    * The Manufacturer owes the Supplier **90** - components, 60 days
    * The Distributor owes the Manufacturer **120** - finished goods, 30 days
    * The Supplier owes the Distributor **40** - logistics, 90 days

    Gross, that is 250 USDC across three invoices. Netted, the loop of 40 cancels, and the rest settles along the original invoices: the Distributor pays the Manufacturer 80, the Manufacturer pays the Supplier 50. **130 moves instead of 250.**
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How it enters">
    Invoices will enter as **claims the debtor approves**: you submit a batch, each debtor approves its own lines; from there it is the standard [planned integration](/netting-api/integration#submit-obligations).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
