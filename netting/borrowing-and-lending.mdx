Payments run both ways between the same institutions - repayments and interest one way, new draws the other, margin calls whenever prices move - and each settles gross. The worst days cost the most: the most falls due in volatile markets, when liquidity is scarcest. Netting is designed to settle the book net instead. Lane: **Future**.

| You will get | How it is designed |
| --- | --- |
| Only your residual funded | Repayments, draws, interest and margin calls clear on [one graph](/netting) |
| Liquidity when it is scarcest | The gap between gross and net is widest in a high-volatility event |
| Your terms, unchanged | Each due date is its deadline; nothing is novated |
| A priced residual | [Programmable collateral vaults](/collateral/vaults) back what doesn't net |

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What changes for you">
    * **Settle the book net.** A loan book is a netting set: repayments, draws, interest, and margin calls between the same participants clear on one graph, and only each institution's residual is funded.
    * **Liquidity freed when it is scarcest.** The gap between gross and net is widest in a high-volatility event, when gross settlement demands the most liquidity just as it is scarcest.
    * **Your terms, unchanged.** Each obligation keeps its own due date as its deadline and clears in the run before it; nothing is novated, and every loan stays between the two parties who signed it.
    * **The residual, handled.** Whatever doesn't net takes a priced backstop from programmable collateral vaults, with an early exit any time before the deadline - so the worst case is what you pay today.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A day of flow">
    Three institutions, one settlement date, USDC on Base:

    * The Fund owes the Bank **100** - a loan repayment
    * The Bank owes the Desk **70** - a draw on a committed credit line
    * The Desk owes the Fund **50** - interest on its credit line

    Gross, that is 220 USDC across three payments. Netted, the loop of 50 cancels, and the rest settles along the original debts: the Fund pays the Bank 50, the Bank pays the Desk 20. **70 moves instead of 220.**
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How it enters">
    Each repayment, draw, or margin call will enter as a **signed debt** from the institution that owes it, or as a batch from the lending market that originated it, which each borrower approves line by line; from there it is the standard [planned integration](/netting-api/integration#submit-obligations).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
